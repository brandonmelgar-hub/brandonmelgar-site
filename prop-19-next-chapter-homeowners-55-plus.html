Next Chapter · Homeowners 55+

Your home took care of your family. Now it can take care of you.

Prop 19, downsizing, and moving closer to family, explained in plain words by someone who grew up here.

If you are 55 or older and own your home in California, Prop 19 lets you sell it and keep your low property tax on your next home, anywhere in the state, up to three times. On a typical Valley move from a long held house into something simpler, that can mean $6,000 to $10,000 a year that stays in your pocket.

Most people I talk with are not in a hurry to move, and that is exactly when this conversation is worth having. Knowing your options now means nobody in your family has to guess later.

Prop 19 if you move

  • Who: homeowners 55 and older, severely disabled homeowners of any age, and victims of wildfire or natural disaster.
  • Where: your new home can be anywhere in California.
  • When: buy or build within two years of selling, before or after the sale.
  • If it costs more: you keep your old tax base and only the difference is added.
  • How often: up to three times.
  • The form: BOE-19-B, filed with the assessor in the new home's county within three years.

Prop 19 if the home passes to your children

The rules changed in 2021, and a lot of families still plan around the old ones. Here is how it works now.

  • It has to be your main home. Rentals and second homes no longer qualify.
  • Your child moves in as their main home within one year and files the homeowners' exemption.
  • Your low tax is protected up to your taxable value plus $1,044,586 (transfers through February 15, 2027). Anything above that is added.
  • If your child later rents it out or moves out, the tax resets.
  • A living trust can qualify. A home held in an LLC does not.

Example: a $210,000 tax base on a home now worth $1,300,000. If your son moves in, his tax is about $3,065 a year instead of about $15,600. That is about $12,535 a year your family keeps.

Questions families ask me

Can I take my property tax base to a more expensive home?

Yes. If the new home costs more than the one you sold, you keep your old tax base and only the difference is added on top. Example: a $210,000 tax base, you sell for $1,100,000 and buy for $1,300,000. Your new taxable value is about $410,000, not $1,300,000.

How many times can I use Prop 19?

Homeowners 55 and older can transfer their tax base up to three times in their lifetime. Each move needs its own claim form.

Do I have to stay in Los Angeles County?

No. Since April 1, 2021 the transfer works anywhere in California, so a move from Studio City to San Diego, Palm Springs or closer to your kids still qualifies.

What form do I file, and when?

Form BOE-19-B, filed with the assessor in the county where your new home is, within three years of buying it. Filing late only gives relief going forward, so I put the deadline in writing for every client.

Do my children keep my low property tax if they inherit the house?

Only if it was your main home and one of them moves in as their main home within a year and files the homeowners' exemption. The protection covers your taxable value plus $1,044,586 (for transfers from February 16, 2025 through February 15, 2027). Rentals and second homes no longer qualify.

Does Prop 19 help if I move into assisted living?

Not directly. The tax transfer only applies when you buy or build a new home you live in. If a parent is moving into care, talk with your estate attorney and CPA before changing the deed or the homeowners' exemption. I am glad to help you get those answers.
