North Hollywood

North Hollywood is the Valley's most in demand entry point right now, and the reason is simple: it is one of the few places here where you can genuinely live without a car for stretches of the week. The Metro B and G lines meet at the NoHo station, and the Arts District has built a real walkable core around it.

What it costs. The median sale price across North Hollywood ran about $860K over the three months to July 2026, down roughly 3% year over year. Inside the NoHo Arts District specifically, single family homes routinely trade in the $900K to $1.3M range, with a median listing price closer to $954,500 in a spring 2026 snapshot.

It is not one market. North Hollywood covers a lot of ground and the difference between blocks is real. The Arts District carries a premium for walkability and transit. Push north or west and the same square footage costs meaningfully less. NoHo also borders Valley Village and Valley Glen, and buyers routinely cross those lines without realizing the pricing changes.

What it is like. Theaters, a genuine arts scene, restaurants that stay open, and housing stock that runs from 1920s bungalows through mid century tract to new construction and small lot subdivisions. It is more urban than most of the Valley and that is the appeal.

Who it fits. First time buyers who want equity and transit, creatives who want to be near the work, and investors who understand that transit adjacency in Los Angeles is not going to get less valuable.

Common questions

What is the median home price in North Hollywood?

About $860,000 over the three months to July 2026, down roughly 3.3% from the same period a year earlier. Single family homes in the NoHo Arts District specifically run higher, generally $900,000 to $1.3M.

Is the NoHo Arts District walkable?

Yes, unusually so for the Valley. It is roughly a square mile built around performance and visual arts venues, with the Metro B and G lines meeting at the North Hollywood station.

Is North Hollywood a good investment?

It is one of the more competitive Valley markets precisely because of the transit and the arts district, and it still sits well below Studio City and Toluca Lake on price. That combination is what buyers are responding to.

What is the difference between North Hollywood and Valley Village?

They border each other and share a community plan area, but Valley Village is smaller, quieter and more residential, and it generally prices higher. People mix them up constantly, which is how buyers overpay and sellers underprice.
