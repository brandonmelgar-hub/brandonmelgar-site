Why We Hand Picked Our Kitchen Slabs In Person, Two Weeks From Listing

We are about two weeks from listing our North Hollywood flip on Willowcrest, and today's job was standing in a slab yard picking out the actual kitchen stone in person, piece by piece, from the same block, so the veining flows across the counters instead of fighting itself. That one decision is the difference between a kitchen that reads custom and one that reads cheap, and it costs nothing extra to get right.

The brick facade is finished. The original hardwood floors are sanded down to raw wood, ready for their finish coat. And today we picked the stone that is going to sit on top of the cabinets for the next few decades. None of that shows up in a quick before and after, but it is exactly the kind of decision that determines whether a flip sells at asking or over it.

Where the Willowcrest flip stands right now

Two weeks out from hitting the market, this is where things are: brick facade complete, original hardwood sanded to raw and staged for refinishing, and the kitchen stone selected in person this week. Back on August 31, we were still finishing tile, setting cabinets, and starting the exterior brick. Every one of those is now done or nearly done, which is what "two weeks out" actually looks like on the ground.

Why you pick slabs in person instead of off a sample chip

A sample chip the size of a coaster tells you almost nothing about how a full slab of natural stone will look on a 10 foot run of counter. Every slab is different, and the veining, the movement in the stone, only reads correctly if consecutive pieces come from the same block, what stone yards call the same family. Pick slabs at random and the veining jumps and fights itself at every seam. Pick them in person, matched from the same family, and the pattern flows like it was always one continuous piece of stone. It is a small thing to walk the yard yourself, and it is the single biggest tell between a flip that was finished carefully and one that was finished fast.

What this means if you are buying a flipped home

Walk into any renovated kitchen and look at the seams in the counter. If the veining lines up and flows from one slab to the next, someone stood in a yard and chose those pieces on purpose. If it jumps or clashes at the seam, it was picked from a catalog without anyone looking at the actual stone. That single detail tells you more about the quality of a flip than almost anything else in the kitchen.

Why does slab veining matter in a kitchen renovation?

Natural stone slabs vary piece to piece. Choosing consecutive slabs from the same block, the same family, keeps the veining flowing across seams instead of clashing. It is a detail most buyers notice without knowing why a kitchen looks expensive.

What stage is the Willowcrest flip in right now?

As of this update, the brick facade is finished, the original hardwood floors are sanded to raw and staged for refinishing, and the kitchen stone has been selected in person. The project is roughly two weeks from listing.

Should you pick your own stone slabs when renovating?

If the budget allows it, yes. Seeing the actual slab in person, rather than a sample chip, is the only way to control veining and movement, and it is often the difference between a kitchen that looks custom and one that looks assembled.

Where is this flip located?

It is on Willowcrest in North Hollywood, the same project documented in the August 31 flip diary post covering tile, cabinets, and the start of the exterior brick.
