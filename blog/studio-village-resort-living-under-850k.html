Studio Village: Six Pools, Tennis Courts, and Studio City Under $850K

Studio Village is a Studio City townhome community where the HOA covers six pools, five spas, and two tennis courts. There is a remodeled 2 bedroom listed at $819,000 right now that backs onto the LA River walk.

Most people looking in Studio City under a million assume they are choosing between a small condo and nothing. Studio Village is the answer almost nobody brings up, and I think that is because townhome communities do not photograph like estates so they get skipped.

What the HOA actually includes

Six pools. Five spas. Two tennis courts. For a community of this size that is an unusual amount of shared amenity, and it is the single most important thing to understand about the value here.

Think about what a private pool costs to install and then maintain in Los Angeles, and what it does to your insurance and your water bill. Studio Village residents get six of them, maintained, without any of that landing on them individually. That is the trade a well run HOA is supposed to give you, and here it delivers.

The current listing as a benchmark

$819,000 for a remodeled 2 bedroom townhome. End unit, which means one less shared wall and usually better light. Private garage, which in Studio City is worth more than people from other cities expect. And it backs onto the LA River walk.

That last detail is the one I would point out on a showing. The river walk is a genuine amenity on this stretch, not a back fence you ignore. People actually use it, walking and running and biking, and having it directly behind you is different from being six blocks away.

Townhome versus condo versus house

This is the distinction that decides whether Studio Village fits you.

A condo generally gets you a unit in a larger building, often with neighbors above and below. A townhome like these gets you your own structure, usually multi level, with your own entrance and garage. It lives much closer to a house than a condo does, without the price of land underneath it.

What you give up is a private yard and full control over the exterior. What you get back is the amenity package and somebody else handling the roof.

Who this fits

Buyers who want space and a garage in Studio City, who use a pool or a tennis court often enough that shared access is worth real money, and who do not need a private yard.

It also fits people trading down out of a larger house who want to stop maintaining a property but are not ready to move into a building.

The honest tradeoff

HOA dues are a real monthly number and you should underwrite them like part of the payment, because your lender will. Ask for the HOA's reserve study and recent meeting minutes before you remove contingencies. Six pools, five spas, and two tennis courts are wonderful right up until they need replacing, and the reserves tell you whether that is funded or whether it is coming as a special assessment.

That is not a reason to avoid the community. It is a reason to read the documents, which is true of every HOA anywhere.
