Why I Show Up to My Sellers' Inspections

A listing I repped in Studio City went into escrow in 12 days, over asking, and one of the reasons is simple: I was standing in the house for the buyer's inspection instead of waiting for the report to land in my inbox.

Most listing agents treat the buyer's inspection as the other side's event. They wait it out, then react to whatever shows up in the write up days later. I do the opposite. I show up.

Why being there matters

When I am physically in the crawl space, the attic, or standing next to the inspector at the water heater, I see everything firsthand instead of secondhand through a PDF. That means I know exactly what is minor, what is normal wear, and what is actually worth a line item before the report is even finished being written.

It also means I can protect my seller in the negotiation that follows. A repair request written by a buyer's agent who was not there, off a report they skimmed, tends to ask for more than it should. A repair request written after I was in the room with the inspector tends to get real.

What happened on this one

This inspection came back clean. No structural surprises, no deferred maintenance nobody knew about, nothing that turns into a week of back and forth. Because of that, the repair request on this deal is going to be minimal, and the file keeps moving instead of stalling out over line items that were never going to hold up anyway.

That is the whole reason this listing went from live to in escrow in 12 days, over asking. Good pricing and good presentation get you the offer. Being present through the contingency period is what keeps that offer intact.

The takeaway for sellers

If you are interviewing agents to list your home, ask them one question: will you be at the buyer's inspection? A lot of agents will not, either because they are juggling too many files or because they simply do not see it as their job once the home is under contract. I see it as one of the most important hours of the entire transaction.

Thinking of selling in Studio City or anywhere in the Valley? I would rather show you how this works on your file than explain it in the abstract. Reach out.

Why does the listing agent attend the buyer's inspection?

Being there means I see every issue firsthand instead of reading about it secondhand in a report. That firsthand knowledge is what lets me push back on an inflated repair request and keep the negotiation grounded in what the home actually needs.

Does a clean inspection mean there is no repair request at all?

Not always, but it usually means the request stays small and reasonable. A clean report gives a buyer's agent less to work with, which is exactly why the repair request on this deal is expected to be minimal.

How fast can a well priced Studio City listing go into escrow?

This one took 12 days from list to accepted offer, over asking. Timelines vary by pocket and price point, but proper pricing, presentation, and being present through the process are the three levers that consistently move a listing faster.
