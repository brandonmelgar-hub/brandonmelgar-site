LA County Property Taxes 2026 to 2027: Due Dates, Penalties and the $7,000 Exemption Valley Homeowners Miss

If you own a home in the Valley, your first 2026 to 2027 LA County property tax installment is due now and goes delinquent at the close of business on Thursday, December 10, 2026. Pay late and you add a 10% penalty. The second installment is due February 1, 2027 and goes delinquent on Monday, April 12, 2027 (April 10 falls on a Saturday), with a 10% penalty plus a $10 cost.

This applies whether you live in Studio City, Sherman Oaks, North Hollywood, Encino or Burbank, since they are all in LA County. The county mailed about 2.4 million secured tax bills on or before November 1. Here is what is on yours, and the two things new owners miss most.

The 2026 to 2027 dates

  • 1st installment: due November 1, 2026, delinquent after close of business Thursday, December 10, 2026
  • 2nd installment: due February 1, 2027, delinquent after close of business Monday, April 12, 2027
  • Late penalty: 10% on each installment, plus a $10 cost on the 2nd
  • Unpaid by June 30: taxes default on July 1, with a $15 redemption fee and 1.5% a month on the base tax

How the bill works

Under Prop 13, the general tax levy is 1% of your assessed value, and your bill adds lines for voted indebtedness (local bonds voters approved) and direct assessments, which vary by area. Your assessed value can only go up a maximum of 2% a year while you own the home, but the county notes your total bill can rise by more than that when new bonds or assessments are added. As a simple illustration, a home assessed at $1,000,000 pays $10,000 in the 1% general levy before bonds and assessments. Check your own bill for the real numbers.

The $7,000 homeowners' exemption

If the home is your primary residence as of January 1, you can take $7,000 off your assessed value. The county says that saves about $75 a year. It sounds small, but it is free money and plenty of new owners never file. To get 100% of it, file by February 15. File after February 15 but before December 10 and you get 80%. The Assessor's office handles it at (213) 974 3415.

Just bought? Watch for the supplemental bill

When you buy, your home is reassessed as of the first day of the month after the sale. The county then sends a supplemental tax bill, generally 3 months to a year after you close, and it comes in addition to your regular bill with its own due dates. Your lender usually does not pay it from your impound account. If you closed between January 1 and May 31, you may get two. Budget for it the day you get your keys.

How to pay

Pay online at propertytax.lacounty.gov until 11:59pm Pacific on the delinquency date. eCheck is free; credit and debit cards cost 2.22% (minimum $1.49). You can also mail it to P.O. Box 54018, Los Angeles, CA 90054, as long as it is postmarked by the delinquency date.

If you are 55+ and thinking about moving

Your low Prop 13 base is one of the most valuable things you own. Prop 19 lets owners 55 and older take it with them to a replacement home anywhere in California. See my Next Chapter page for homeowners 55+, and if you are inheriting a parent's home, read the Prop 19 rule most families miss.

When are LA County property taxes due in 2026?

The first installment of the 2026 to 2027 bill is due November 1, 2026 and becomes delinquent after close of business on Thursday, December 10, 2026. The second is due February 1, 2027 and becomes delinquent after close of business on Monday, April 12, 2027.

What is the penalty for paying LA County property tax late?

10% of the installment, plus a $10 cost on the second installment. If taxes are still unpaid on June 30, they default, with a $15 redemption fee and 1.5% a month on the base tax.

How much does the homeowners' exemption save in LA County?

It reduces your assessed value by $7,000, which the county says saves about $75 a year. File by February 15 for the full exemption.

What is a supplemental property tax bill?

A separate bill sent after you buy, based on your new assessed value from the first of the month after the purchase. It usually arrives 3 months to a year after closing, and lenders usually do not pay it.
