Design Flop or Design Pop? What Omake in Studio City Teaches About Materials That Last

Omake, the coffee shop at 12437 Ventura Blvd in Studio City, is a design pop on day one: curved travertine, a walnut header with the light built right in, frameless doors and a bathroom that steals the show. But in my video I pointed out that the floor already looks scratched about six weeks in. That is the real lesson for anyone remodeling in the Valley: beautiful is the easy part, lasting is the job, and the materials that look best are not always the ones that hold up to daily traffic.

I posted this one as a design flop or design pop question, and I want to be clear: I like this room. It is one of the most thought through interiors on the Boulevard right now. I am writing about it because the same choices people admire in a cafe are the ones they bring home to their own kitchens and bathrooms.

What Omake is

Omake sits on the 12400 block of Ventura Blvd, between Laurel Canyon and Whitsett. When What Now covered the opening in April 2026, owner Iskuhi Kalantaryan described it as a coffee and tea education concept, with tasting experiences, multi roastery coffees and ceremonial matcha from Japan, and the article notes the space was built with natural stone, walnut wood and microcement. The Infatuation describes a five course coffee omakase. It is also on my list of 22 coffee shops in Studio City.

The design pops

  • Curved travertine. Travertine is a warm, natural stone, and curving it takes real fabrication work. It is the kind of detail that makes a room feel expensive.
  • A walnut header with the light built in. Integrating lighting into millwork instead of hanging a fixture is a clean, custom move. It is the same idea we used on our North Hollywood project, where every light was chosen or made for the house.
  • Frameless doors. Less hardware, cleaner lines.
  • The bathroom. In a small commercial space, a bathroom that gets this much attention tells you the owners cared about every corner.

The flop: why the floor matters most

In the video I noted the floor already looks scratched after about six weeks. A cafe floor takes more foot traffic in a month than most homes take in a year, so it is a stress test. But the lesson carries over to your house: the surface you walk on every day is the one that has to be chosen for durability first and looks second.

A few things worth knowing before you pick materials at home:

  • Travertine and marble are calcite based stones. They are softer than granite or quartz, and acids like coffee, wine or citrus can etch them. They need to be sealed and maintained. Beautiful on a wall or a fireplace, more work on a kitchen counter.
  • Walnut is a softer hardwood than oak. On the Janka hardness scale, black walnut rates around 1,010 compared to about 1,360 for white oak, so it dents more easily. Great for millwork and accents, riskier on a busy floor.
  • Finish matters as much as material. Whatever you choose, ask what sealer or topcoat it gets, how often it needs to be redone, and what scratches will look like in a year.

Why a Realtor is writing about a coffee shop

Two reasons. First, buyers notice wear. When I walk a listing, the finishes that look tired are the ones buyers mentally subtract money for, even if the house is otherwise beautiful. Choosing materials that last is how a remodel keeps its value. That is the same thinking behind why we hand picked our kitchen slabs in person.

Second, location. Omake sits right where two Studio City pockets meet. South of the Boulevard is the Silver Triangle, the most walkable of the 22 Studio City pockets, where homes run around $2.3M in 2026, with Trader Joe's, restaurants and the Fryman Canyon trails all on foot. North of the Boulevard is the Whitsett and Laurel Canyon pocket. Living a short walk from spots like this is a big part of what people pay for on either side.

Is travertine a good choice for a kitchen?

Travertine is beautiful but it is a calcite based stone, softer than granite or quartz, and acids like coffee, wine or citrus can etch it. It works if you seal it and accept some patina. For a hard working counter, many owners pick a harder surface.

Does walnut scratch easily?

Black walnut is softer than oak, about 1,010 on the Janka hardness scale versus about 1,360 for white oak, so it dents and scratches more easily on floors. It is a great choice for millwork, cabinetry and accents.
