Buy First or Sell First? The Prop 19 Timing Rule That Changes the Math for Valley Homeowners 55+

If you are 55 or older and moving under Prop 19, buying your next home after you sell gives you more room than buying first. Buy before the sale and the new home can cost up to 100% of what your old one sold for without raising your tax base. Buy in the first year after the sale and the line moves to 105%. Buy in the second year and it is 110%. On a Valley house that sells for $1,100,000, that is up to $110,000 of extra room.

This is the question I get right after "can I take my tax base with me?" The answer to that one is yes, anywhere in California, up to three times. Here is the full picture on that. But the order you do things in changes the math, and almost nobody tells you that part.

The rule comes from the State Board of Equalization, in its December 11, 2024 guidance to every county assessor in California (Letter to Assessors No. 2024/044).

Stay under the line and your old tax base comes with you as it is. Go over the line and only the amount over the line is added to your tax base. You never lose the whole benefit for buying something nicer.

A Valley example with real math

Say you bought your Studio City or Sherman Oaks house decades ago and your tax base today is $210,000. The house sells for $1,100,000, and the home you really want costs $1,300,000, maybe a single level house closer to the kids, or one with a guest house.

Same two houses, same family. Buying in the second year after the sale instead of before it is about $1,320 a year lower, every year you live there. And all three are a long way from $15,600.

Now say the next home is $1,150,000 instead. Buy it before you sell and $50,000 is added, about $3,120 a year. Buy it within a year after you sell and it is under the 105% line, so nothing is added and you stay at about $2,520 a year.

Approximate. Real bills include local assessments, the assessor adjusts the original value for inflation, and the County Assessor makes the final call.

So should everyone sell first?

Not always. The tax math leans toward selling first. Real life does not always cooperate. Here is how I walk families through it:

Selling first works when

  • You have somewhere to land: a rent back from your buyer, a short lease, or family nearby.
  • The home you want costs more than the one you are selling, so the extra 5% or 10% of room actually matters.
  • You want your full sale proceeds in hand before you commit to the next house.

Buying first works when

  • Moving twice would be hard on you or on a parent.
  • The next home costs the same or less than the one you are selling, so the 100% line is not a problem anyway.
  • You can buy without the sale money, with cash or with financing your lender approves. That is a question for your lender, and I will connect you with people I trust.

Three dates that matter more than the price

  1. Your 55th birthday. You have to be at least 55 on the day your original home sells. If you are close, the closing date matters. Only one spouse needs to qualify.
  2. The two year window. The new home has to be bought, or built and finished, within two years before or after the sale. The Board says there is no extension for hardship or construction delays.
  3. The three year claim. File the claim (form BOE-19-B) with the assessor in the county of your new home within three years of buying it. File late and you only get the lower tax from that year forward. Past years are not refunded.

One more thing people miss: both homes have to be your main home. The one you sell has to have qualified for the homeowners' exemption, and the new one has to be your main home when you file.

Questions families ask

If I am 55 or older, should I buy my next home before or after I sell?

Under Prop 19 the timing changes how much room you get. If you buy before you sell, the new home can cost up to 100% of what your old home sold for without raising your tax base. Buy within the first year after the sale and the limit is 105%. Buy in the second year and it is 110%. Above the limit, only the amount over the limit is added to your tax base.

How long do I have to buy a replacement home under Prop 19?

Two years before or two years after the sale of your original home. If you build, the new home has to be finished inside that window. The State Board of Equalization says there is no extension for hardship or delays.

Do I have to be 55 when I buy or when I sell?

You have to be at least 55 on the date your original home sells. If you are married, only one spouse needs to qualify, and a younger spouse does not block the transfer.

What happens if I file the Prop 19 claim late?

The claim is due to the County Assessor within three years of buying or finishing the new home. A late claim only helps from the year you file it forward. Past years are not refunded.
