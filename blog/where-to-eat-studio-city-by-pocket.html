Studio City · Living Here

Where to Eat in Studio City, Pocket by Pocket

Almost all of Studio City's food sits on five commercial strips: Ventura Boulevard, Ventura Place, the Tujunga Avenue village, two small nodes on Moorpark, and the Vineland gateway on the east end. Everything else is residential. Seven of Studio City's pockets have no walkable food at all, and people pay a premium to live in them.

I had a conversation this week about the sushi, the matcha and the burgers here, and it turned into something more useful than a restaurant list. Because where the food is tells you almost everything about how a Studio City street lives.

Two houses can be a mile apart, cost the same, and offer completely different daily lives. One walks to coffee. The other is on a canyon road where the nearest anything is a five minute drive. Neither is better. But nobody tells you which one you are buying.

The five strips, and why it matters

Studio City is not evenly built. The commercial zoning runs in narrow lines and everything else is houses. Once you see the lines, the map makes sense.

  • Ventura Boulevard. The spine. Sushi Row, the coffee, the delis, most of the restaurants in this piece.
  • Ventura Place. A one block spur at Laurel Canyon that punches far above its size.
  • Tujunga Village. The only true walkable village street, and smaller than its reputation.
  • Moorpark Street. Two small neighborhood nodes, one near Colfax and one near Whitsett.
  • The east gateway. Vineland, Lankershim and Cahuenga, where Studio City meets North Hollywood.

Sushi Row is the real thing

This is what Studio City is known for nationally, and it earned it. Teru Sushi at 11940 Ventura opened in 1979 and effectively started it. Asanebo sits directly across the street at 11941 and is the best meal on the stretch. Sushi Katsu-ya at 11680, the 1997 original, is credited with inventing the spicy tuna crispy rice that is now on menus in every state.

Add Leona's Sushi House at 11814, Japanese Peruvian and Michelin recommended, Yume Sushi at 12254, Iroha at 12953 with the garden walkway, KazuNori at 11288 for hand rolls with no reservation, and Sugarfish at Sportsmen's Lodge.

One correction worth making, because half the guides online still get it wrong: Sushi Nozawa closed in 2012. Sugarfish grew out of it. If a list you are reading names Nozawa as a stop, that list is fifteen years old.

Five matcha bars on one boulevard

This is the part that surprised me when I actually counted. Inside about 1.2 miles of Ventura Boulevard: Café Matcha at 12070, Coffee and Plants at 12334 with ceremonial grade and house made plant milks, rōk coffee and tea at 12265, Omake at 12437 doing guided tastings, and Blank Street at 13273, which opened in August 2026 as their first West Coast location.

Add Alfred on Ventura Place, Aroma in Tujunga Village, Civil Coffee at Sportsmen's Lodge, and the small off boulevard shops on Moorpark, and the density is genuinely unusual for the Valley.

The casual stuff

Carney's at 12601 Ventura serves burgers out of two 1940s railroad cars, which is still the most Studio City thing on the boulevard. Laurel Tavern at 11938 grinds its own. HiHo at Sportsmen's Lodge does the Wagyu smash. Oy Bar at 12446 Moorpark is off boulevard and worth the detour.

For pizza, Prince Street at 12067 Ventura Place brought the SoHo square slice west. Caioti in Tujunga Village is the one with the salad expectant mothers order to induce labor, which sounds like a myth until you talk to someone in the neighborhood.

For tacos, El Ruso at 10624 Ventura took over the old Roadside Taco space in July 2026. Sonoran style, flour tortillas pressed to order. It is the best new thing on the east end.

And Art's Delicatessen at 12224 has been there since 1957.

Now the part that actually matters if you are buying

Here is the honest map. Not which pocket has the best food, but whether you can walk to any.

Every one of those seven is quiet, private, and priced accordingly. People do not end up there by accident, they choose it. The tradeoff is that a coffee is a drive, not a walk. That is worth knowing before you write an offer, not after.

What I would actually tell a buyer

Decide which of the two lives you want before you look at houses, because it narrows the map fast.

If you want to walk out your door and end up somewhere, you are looking at Silver Triangle, Tujunga Village, Footbridge Square, The Grove, and the boulevard-adjacent edges. If you want quiet and a view and do not mind driving four minutes for everything, the hillside pockets are where your money goes further per square foot.

What you should not do is buy in the hills expecting a walkable life, or buy on the boulevard edge expecting silence. Both are real complaints I have heard from people who bought before anyone drew them this map.

What is Studio City's Sushi Row?

A concentration of sushi restaurants along Ventura Boulevard, densest between Coldwater Canyon and Laurel Canyon. Teru Sushi started it in 1979. Asanebo, Sushi Katsu-ya, Leona's, Yume, Iroha, KazuNori and Sugarfish are the current core. Sushi Nozawa, still named in many older guides, closed in 2012.

Which Studio City neighborhood is the most walkable?

Silver Triangle. You can reach Trader Joe's, Vons and the Ventura Boulevard restaurants on foot. Tujunga Village has its own walkable street, and Footbridge Square connects to the boulevard by a pedestrian bridge over the LA River.

Are there parts of Studio City with no restaurants nearby?

Yes, seven pockets have no walkable food: Fryman Canyon, Longridge Estates, Alta Avenida Loop, Coldwater-Goodland, Wrightwood Estates, Laurelwood and the Doñas, and Sunswept Hill. All are residential hillside areas with no commercial zoning. That privacy is a large part of what buyers there are paying for.

Does walkability change what a house is worth in Studio City?

It changes what kind of buyer competes for it. Walkable pockets draw people who want to leave the car home, which supports demand on smaller lots. Hillside pockets trade walkability for privacy, views and land, and generally get you more square footage and lot for the money. The two are priced by different logic.
