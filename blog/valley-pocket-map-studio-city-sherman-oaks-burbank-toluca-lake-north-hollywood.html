The Valley Pocket Map

Studio Village homes run around $1.0M. Laurelwood, the Doñas streets up the hill, runs around $3.5M. Both are Studio City, and that gap is why the ZIP code tells you almost nothing. So I mapped every pocket in five Valley neighborhoods, Studio City, Sherman Oaks, Burbank, Toluca Lake and North Hollywood, 137 in all, and you can tap yours below.

I grew up in the Woodbridge neighborhood of Studio City and I have been selling here for nine years. The question I get more than any other is some version of "what is my house worth," and the honest answer always starts with "which pocket are you in?" Two homes a few blocks apart can sit in completely different markets. This map is my answer to that question, in one place.

Same city, different market

Studio City alone shows how much the pocket matters. Studio Village, the 1970s townhouse complex with pools, runs around $1.0M. North Weddington runs around $1.1M. Walk south past Ventura and up into Wrightwood Estates and you are around $2.8M, and Laurelwood around $3.5M. Sherman Oaks does the same thing, with Magnolia Woods around $915K and Hidden Woods around $1.4M. Pricing a home off the city average puts you in the wrong market before you start.

Where the borders come from

There is no official list of pockets anywhere. The names on this map follow the neighborhood map that neighbors already use on Nextdoor, because those are the names people actually say. The borders are approximate, so if you live right on an edge, your street could sit a block either way. Use it to find your pocket and your neighbors, not for school, tax or legal decisions.

Why do pocket prices vary so much?

Lot size, hills versus flats, walkability to Ventura Blvd and the type of home all change from one pocket to the next. In Studio City that spread runs from around $1.0M in Studio Village to around $3.5M in Laurelwood.
