Inside the First Mercedes-Benz Studio in America (and Why It's in Beverly Hills)

Mercedes-Benz just opened its first Studio in the United States, and they put it in Beverly Hills. It is not a dealership. It is a private space where a client sits down and designs a Maybach from scratch, and the ZIP code was not an accident. That room is full of the same kind of buyer who is out here building custom homes across LA.

I got to spend time in the space recently, and it stuck with me for reasons that had nothing to do with cars. This was not a showroom with a sticker price taped to the windshield. It was closer to a design office. You sit down, you go through finishes, materials, colors, options that do not exist on a lot anywhere, and you build a car the same way you would sit with an architect and build a house.

Not a dealership, a design studio

The word "studio" is doing real work in the name. There is no inventory to walk. There is no salesperson pointing you toward what is already on the ground. The whole model is built around one person, one build, start to finish. That is a completely different experience than pulling into a dealership lot, and it is meant to be.

Why Beverly Hills

Mercedes had the entire country to choose from for the first one of these, and they chose Beverly Hills. That is not a random pin on a map. It is where the client who wants a Maybach built to their own specification already lives, or already shops, or already drives through on a normal Tuesday. Brands do not put their most exclusive concept in a city by accident. They put it where the demand already exists.

The real estate parallel

I could not stop thinking about how similar that room felt to the conversations I have with buyers building or renovating luxury homes across LA. Same instinct. Same willingness to wait longer and pay more for something built to spec instead of picked off a lot. The person custom ordering a Maybach in Beverly Hills is very often the same profile of person who is not interested in a home that already exists exactly as is either. They want the version built for them.

The honest tradeoff

Here is the part worth saying plainly. This is not a place most people will ever walk into, and it is not trying to be. It is an experience built for a very narrow slice of buyer, and for everyone else it functions more as brand theater than as a place you shop. That is fine and it is by design, but it is worth naming instead of pretending it is something it is not.

What I take from it as an agent is simpler than the car itself. The appetite for custom, for built to order, for something nobody else has, is alive and well at the top of this market. Whether that shows up in a Maybach or in a hillside rebuild, it is the same buyer making the same decision.

If you are thinking about building or buying something custom in LA and want to talk through what that actually looks like, reach out. No pressure, just a real conversation.

What is the Mercedes-Benz Studio?

It is Mercedes-Benz's first Studio location in the United States, located in Beverly Hills. It functions as a private design space rather than a dealership, where a client works through finishes and options to build a Maybach from scratch.

Can anyone visit the Studio?

It is built around a very specific, high end client rather than general walk in shopping, which is part of the point. It is an exclusive experience, not a browsing showroom.

Why does a real estate agent care about a car showroom?

Because the buyer in that room is often the same buyer I work with on custom and luxury homes across LA. Watching how a different luxury brand treats its top clients is useful, whether the product is a car or a house.
