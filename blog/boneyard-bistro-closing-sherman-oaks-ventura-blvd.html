Sherman Oaks · Ventura Boulevard

Boneyard Bistro Is Closing After 21 Years. Here Is What It Says About Sherman Oaks

Boneyard Bistro, the BBQ and bistro spot at 13539 Ventura Blvd, served its last meal after 21 years in Sherman Oaks. A single restaurant closing is sad, but it does not signal a neighborhood in decline. The opposite is true. That stretch of Ventura Boulevard is one of the most valuable walkable dining corridors in the San Fernando Valley, and the demand for the homes a few blocks off it is exactly why a space like that one never stays empty for long.

I had my last dinner there this week, so this one is personal. But I sell homes in this neighborhood for a living, and the closing is worth looking at through that lens too, because the Boulevard is one of the biggest reasons Sherman Oaks holds its value.

Why the Boulevard is a real estate story, not just a food story

When buyers ask me what they are paying for in Sherman Oaks, a lot of it comes down to one thing: you can walk to Ventura Boulevard. Coffee, dinner, a workout, a haircut, all of it within a few minutes of home. That walkability is not a lifestyle perk you enjoy and forget. It is a value driver that shows up in the price of every home south of the Boulevard and in the pockets just north of it. A restaurant closing after 21 years is a reminder of how much life happens on that street, not a sign that the street is fading.

Turnover on a great corridor is normal, and it is healthy

The best commercial stretches in Los Angeles are always turning over. A 21 year run is a long, successful life for a restaurant, and when a well located space on Ventura opens up, it gets filled, usually by something new that pulls its own crowd. That churn is what keeps a dining corridor current instead of frozen. The neighborhoods that lose value are the ones where storefronts go dark and stay dark. That is not Ventura Boulevard, and it is not Sherman Oaks.

What this means if you own near the Boulevard

If you own a home within walking distance of this stretch, the closing changes nothing about what your home is worth. The demand that supports your value is the corridor itself, not any single tenant on it. If anything, a marquee space becoming available is the kind of thing that brings the next draw to the street, which is a tailwind for the homes around it, not a headwind.

The point

I am going to miss Boneyard. Twenty one years in one spot is a real legacy, and Sherman Oaks is better for having had it. But if you are reading a restaurant closing as a signal about the neighborhood, read it the right way. The Boulevard is the asset. It is why people pay to live here, and it is not going anywhere.

Does a restaurant closing hurt nearby home values?

No. A single business closing on a strong, walkable corridor like Ventura Boulevard does not move home values. What supports value is the corridor as a whole: the walkability, the density of shops and restaurants, and the demand to live near it. A well located space that opens up almost always gets filled.

What makes Sherman Oaks homes hold their value?

A big part of it is proximity to Ventura Boulevard. Being able to walk to dining, coffee, fitness, and services is one of the most consistent value drivers in the neighborhood, and it shows up in the price of homes both south and just north of the Boulevard.

Where was Boneyard Bistro?

Boneyard Bistro was at 13539 Ventura Blvd in Sherman Oaks. It operated for 21 years before closing, one of the more recognizable dining spots on that stretch of the Boulevard.
