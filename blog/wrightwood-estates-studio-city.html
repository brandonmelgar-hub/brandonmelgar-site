Studio City · The 22 Pockets

Wrightwood Estates

Homes in Wrightwood Estates run around $2.8 million. What that buys is a hillside house on a tight winding road with east facing Valley views toward Universal City, and no sidewalk, no corner store, and nothing at all you can walk to. That is not a flaw in the pocket. It is the entire reason people choose it.

This is one of the 22 pockets of Studio City, and it is one of the least understood. People drive Wrightwood on the way to somewhere else and never register that it is its own neighborhood with its own rules.

What it actually feels like

Tight, winding hill roads. Not the wide graded streets of the flats, and not the gated estate roads of Fryman either. Wrightwood sits in between, which is exactly why it gets skipped in conversation.

The views face east, which matters more than most buyers realize. You are looking out over the Valley toward Universal City. Morning light instead of afternoon glare, and at night you get the whole eastern basin lit up.

The detail almost nobody knows

There is a second way out. Most of these hillside pockets are one road in, one road out, and that is how they feel to live in. Wrightwood has an alternate route up to Mulholland Drive.

That sounds like trivia until you have sat on Ventura Boulevard at 5pm. A second exit changes your daily life in a way no listing photo communicates. It also matters for anyone thinking about fire season and evacuation routes, which is a real consideration in these hills and one buyers ask about more every year.

What $2.8M means here

At that number in Studio City you have real choices, and they are genuinely different lives.

WhereWhat $2.8M gets you
Wrightwood EstatesHillside house, east facing Valley view, winding road, total quiet, a second route out. Nothing walkable.
Silver TriangleFlat lot, walk to Trader Joe's and the boulevard restaurants. No view, more neighbors, more noise.
Longridge EstatesBigger land in a quiet valley by Harvard-Westlake, but you are further from everything and the views are different.

None of those is the right answer. They are three different daily lives at the same price, and most buyers never get shown the comparison.

Who it actually fits

People who want quiet, separation and a view without paying full luxury estate pricing. If you work from home, if you value the drive up more than the walk to coffee, if you want to see the Valley from your living room, this pocket does that.

It does not fit someone who pictures walking out for a matcha. There is no version of Wrightwood where that happens. Every restaurant and coffee shop in Studio City sits on five commercial strips, and none of them run through here. Every trip is a drive.

The thing to do before you write an offer

Drive it twice. Once in the morning and once at 6pm on a weekday. Hillside pockets change character completely between those two moments, and the winding road that felt charming on a Sunday feels different when you are doing it every night in the dark.

Then drive both exits. Take the Mulholland route once so you know it exists and know what it costs you in minutes. That one piece of knowledge is worth more than any comp I could hand you.

How much do homes cost in Wrightwood Estates?

Roughly $2.8 million as of 2026. That is a pocket level figure, not a specific listing. Inventory in a pocket this small moves the number quickly, so ask for this week's actual comps before anchoring on it.

What makes Wrightwood Estates different from other Studio City hillside pockets?

Two things. The views face east toward Universal City rather than south or west, and there is an alternate route up to Mulholland Drive instead of a single way in and out. Most hillside pockets here have one road.

Can you walk anywhere from Wrightwood Estates?

No. There is no commercial zoning in the pocket and no sidewalk network. All of Studio City's food and retail sits on Ventura Boulevard, Ventura Place, Tujunga Village, two small nodes on Moorpark, and the Vineland gateway. Every one of those is a drive from here.

Is Wrightwood Estates a good investment?

It holds the things that stay scarce in Studio City: elevation, a protected view, and quiet. What it lacks is walkability, which is what a growing share of buyers say they want. Which of those wins depends on your timeline, and it is worth talking through against your actual numbers rather than a general rule.
