How to Get Prequalified to Buy a Home in LA: The 3 Things, the 4 Steps, and Exactly What Your Lender Needs

Getting prequalified is not a mountain of paperwork and a hard sell. It is a short conversation about four things: your income, your debt, your FICO score and your savings. It takes about 10 to 15 minutes, there is zero obligation, and you walk away knowing your real number and your next step.

I hear the same thing every week from people in their late twenties and thirties who want to buy: "I'm not ready yet." Most of the time they have never actually checked. So I sat down with Nicole Melgar, a mortgage banker at Arcstone Financial, and asked her to walk through exactly what happens, start to finish.

The 3 things it takes to find out if you can buy

  1. A short call. Nicole asks about your income, your debt, your FICO score and your savings. That's pretty much it, and there's no pressure.
  2. A few documents. You send your ID, your taxes, your bank statements and your pay stubs.
  3. Your numbers and a plan. She tells you your max purchase price and what your mortgage payment would look like.

What your lender actually needs from you

  • A copy of your ID
  • Your last two years of taxes, your W2s or 1099s
  • Your last two months of bank statements
  • Your pay stubs
  • Your okay to check your credit

Most people already have all of this on their phone or in their email.

What if the number isn't what you expected?

This is the part people skip, and it's the most valuable part. If your credit needs work, you build a plan to improve it. If you need more down payment, you find out how much and how long you need to save. The sooner you know, the more time you have. You leave with a game plan, not just a no.

Would you rather know your numbers today, or keep guessing for another year?

The 4 step plan to buy a home in 2027

  1. Get prequalified. Know your numbers and see if anything needs fixing.
  2. Gather your documents. Check your credit, check your debt, and check how much you have saved for the down payment and closing costs.
  3. Get your max price and your real monthly payment. Once your lender has everything, they calculate your max purchase price and show you what your monthly payment will look like, so you can decide what you're comfortable with.
  4. Go shopping. This is where I come in. We find your home.

Questions buyers ask

How long does it take to get prequalified?

Nicole Melgar, the mortgage banker I work with, says the conversation itself takes about 10 to 15 minutes. She asks about your income, your debt, your FICO score and your savings.

What documents do I need to get prequalified?

A copy of your ID, your last two years of taxes (W2s or 1099s), your last two months of bank statements, your pay stubs, and your okay for the lender to check your credit.

Is there any obligation if I get prequalified?

No. Getting prequalified is a conversation, not a commitment. You find out your numbers and leave with a plan, with zero obligation to buy or to use that lender.

What if my numbers are not what I expected?

That is the point of doing it early. If your credit needs work, you build a plan to improve it. If you need more down payment, you learn how much you need and how long it will take to save. You leave with a game plan, not just a no.
