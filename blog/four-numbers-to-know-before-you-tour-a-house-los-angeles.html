The 4 Numbers to Know Before You Tour a Single House in Los Angeles

Before you tour a single house, know four numbers: the most you can borrow, the monthly payment you are actually comfortable with, your down payment, and your interest rate. They move together. At Freddie Mac's 7.18% average 30 year rate (week of November 5, 2026), an $850,000 Valley home with 20% down ($170,000) comes to about $4,607 a month in principal and interest, before property tax and insurance. Change any one of the four and the other three shift.

I posted a short video on this, and it is the conversation I have with almost every buyer. Most people start in the wrong order: they scroll listings, fall for a house, and only then find out what it really costs them each month. Flip it. Get the four numbers first and you shop with confidence instead of guessing.

1. How much house you can afford

This is your ceiling, and a lender sets it from your income, your monthly debts, your credit and your down payment. It comes out of a short prequalification conversation, and I walk through exactly what a lender asks for in how to get prequalified in LA.

One local detail matters here. In Los Angeles County the 2026 conforming loan limit for a single family home is $1,249,125 (FHFA). A loan above that is a jumbo loan, which usually comes with stricter rules. In the Valley that line is close: Burbank's median sale price was $1,176,940 in September 2026, according to Redfin.

2. The monthly payment you are comfortable with

Your approval is not your budget. The lender tells you the most you can borrow. Only you can decide what you want leaving your account every month while you still live your life, save and travel.

Your full payment is more than the loan. It includes principal and interest, property tax, homeowners insurance, HOA dues on a condo or townhome, and mortgage insurance if you put less than 20% down. In California, Prop 13 sets the base property tax at 1% of your purchase price, and local voter approved bonds and assessments are added on top. On an $850,000 home, that 1% base alone is about $708 a month.

3. Your down payment

It does not have to be 20%. FHA allows 3.5% down with a 580 credit score, some conventional programs go to 3%, and VA can be 0% for eligible veterans. I break all of that down in the 20% down payment myth. Here is what the down payment does to the monthly number on an $850,000 home at 7.18% over 30 years:

  • 20% down ($170,000): $680,000 loan, about $4,607 a month principal and interest
  • 3.5% down ($29,750): $820,250 loan, about $5,557 a month principal and interest, before FHA mortgage insurance (same rate shown for comparison; your actual FHA rate may differ)

Less down gets you in sooner. More down lowers the payment. Neither is wrong, it depends which of the four numbers matters most to you. Closing costs come on top of the down payment, so budget for those too.

4. Your interest rate

Freddie Mac's weekly survey for November 5, 2026 put the average 30 year fixed rate at 7.18% and the 15 year at 6.57%. A year earlier the 30 year was 6.22%. That nearly one point gap is real money: on a $680,000 loan, the payment is about $4,174 a month at 6.22% and about $4,607 at 7.18%, a difference of roughly $433 every month.

Keep in mind the survey average assumes 20% down and excellent credit. Your own rate depends on your credit score, down payment, loan type and whether you pay points, which is why I always want my buyers to get a real quote, not a headline number.

How the four numbers fit together

Here is the same math at a real Valley price point. At Burbank's September 2026 median of $1,176,940:

  • Down payment at 20%: $235,388
  • Loan amount: $941,552, under the $1,249,125 LA County conforming limit
  • Rate: 7.18%, the Freddie Mac average for November 5, 2026
  • Principal and interest: about $6,378 a month, before property tax, insurance and any HOA

If that payment is more than you are comfortable with, you have three levers: a lower price, a bigger down payment, or a better rate. That is the whole game, and it is why I want these four numbers on the table before we tour anything.

What are the four numbers to know before buying a house?

Your maximum price (what a lender will approve), the monthly payment you are comfortable with, your down payment, and your interest rate. They depend on each other, so changing one changes the rest.

Is the amount I am approved for the amount I should spend?

Not necessarily. The approval is a ceiling set by the lender. Your comfortable monthly payment, including property tax, insurance and any HOA, is often lower, and that is the number that should guide your search.

How much does the interest rate change my payment?

On a $680,000 loan over 30 years, the payment is about $4,174 a month at 6.22% and about $4,607 at 7.18%, roughly $433 a month for a difference of just under one point.

What is the conforming loan limit in Los Angeles County for 2026?

$1,249,125 for a one unit home, per the FHFA. Loans above that are jumbo loans, which usually have stricter requirements.
