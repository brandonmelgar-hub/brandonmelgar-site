What $1.1 Million Buys in Laguna Niguel

In Laguna Niguel, $1.1 million buys a 4 bedroom, 3 bathroom end unit of roughly 1,800 square feet inside a gated, resort-style community with a panoramic hillside view. In Studio City, the same number often buys a smaller, older home on a tight lot with no view and no amenities. Same money. Very different life.

People always ask me what their money actually buys in Southern California, so let me show you a real one instead of a hypothetical.

This week I hosted an open house at 27327 Sahara Place in Laguna Niguel, listed at $1,099,000. It is a good example of what a little over a million gets you down in South Orange County, and it makes a useful mirror for anyone shopping the same number up in the Valley.

The house itself

Four bedrooms, three bathrooms, right around 1,800 square feet, tucked inside Village Niguel Gardens, a gated community with the pools and grounds handled for you.

What makes this one stand out is that it is an end unit. It shares a single common wall, which in attached living is the difference between hearing your neighbors and forgetting they exist. You get most of the privacy of a detached home with none of the weekend maintenance.

And then there is the view. From the back you are looking at panoramic hillside and treetops, the kind of green, quiet backdrop that makes you forget you are minutes from everything Laguna Niguel and the coast have to offer.

How that compares to the Valley

Around Studio City, $1.1 million often buys a smaller, older home on a tight lot, frequently with no view and no gated amenities. Down in Laguna Niguel that same budget can buy space, a real outlook, and resort-style living.

Neither market is better. They are different, and knowing which one fits your money and your life is the whole game. A Valley buyer is usually buying proximity, studios, the 101, a fifteen minute commute. A South Orange County buyer is usually buying square footage, air, and a view. You are not paying more or less, you are paying for a different thing.

Three things to know at this price point in Laguna Niguel

  • The HOA is doing real work. Gated, amenity-rich communities like this one fold pools, grounds, and exterior maintenance into the dues. That takes a lot off your plate as an owner, and it is worth pricing honestly rather than treating it as a penalty.
  • End units command a premium, and they earn it. Privacy and natural light are worth paying for, and they hold their value on the way back out.
  • Views here are often protected by the terrain itself. A hillside or treetop outlook tends to stay that way for the long haul, which is not something you can count on everywhere.

The point

This is exactly the kind of home I love showing people who assume a budget like this only stretches so far. It stretches further than most people think. You just have to know where to point it.

If you are weighing Orange County against the Valley, or you simply want to know what your number actually buys in either market, reach out. Showing people the real math is the whole reason I do this.

Is $1.1 million a lot for Laguna Niguel?

It is a mid-market number there. It buys a well-located attached home in a gated community with real square footage and, in this case, a protected view. It is not entry level and it is not luxury, it is the middle of the market where most buyers actually live.

What does an end unit actually get you?

One shared wall instead of two, more exterior windows, and more natural light. In attached living that is the single biggest driver of whether a home feels like a house or feels like a unit, and it shows up in resale.

Would that same budget go further in the Valley?

Not in square footage or amenities. In Studio City, $1.1 million typically means a smaller, older detached home on a tight lot. What you gain is location and land. What you give up is space, a view, and the HOA doing your yard work.
