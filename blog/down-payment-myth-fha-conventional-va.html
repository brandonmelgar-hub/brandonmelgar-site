The 20% Down Payment Myth: What FHA, Conventional, and VA Loans Actually Require

You do not need 20% down to buy a house. FHA loans allow as little as 3.5% down, certain conventional loans go as low as 3% down, and VA loans offer 0% down for eligible veterans and active duty service members. The 20% number is a guideline for avoiding mortgage insurance, not a requirement to buy.

This is the myth I hear the most, and it is the one keeping the most people on the sidelines. My sister Nicole is a lender, and we sat down on camera to break down the real numbers so people actually know what they qualify for before they count themselves out.

Where the 20% number actually comes from

The number is not made up, it is just misapplied. Putting 20% down on a conventional loan lets you skip private mortgage insurance, the monthly fee lenders charge to protect themselves when your down payment is smaller. Somewhere along the way that guideline turned into a rumor that 20% is the minimum to buy at all. It never was.

What each loan type actually requires

FHA loans allow 3.5% down with a credit score of 580 or higher. Below that, down to 500, the requirement moves to 10% down. FHA is backed by the federal government, which is why lenders can offer that flexibility.

Conventional loans can go as low as 3% down through programs built for first time buyers, like Fannie Mae HomeReady and Freddie Mac Home Possible. Many standard conventional loans still ask for 5%, so the specific program matters more than the loan type alone.

VA loans offer 0% down for eligible veterans, active duty service members, and surviving spouses. It is one of the strongest benefits available to anyone who qualifies, and it is underused.

What this looks like at a real Valley price point

Numbers make this land better than percentages do. On an $850,000 home, a price point you will find in pockets like Studio Village here in Studio City, the down payment options look like this.

  • 20% down: $170,000
  • FHA at 3.5% down: $29,750
  • Conventional at 3% down: $25,500
  • VA at 0% down: $0

That is not a small gap. That is the difference between years of saving and being able to buy this year.

The part people should not skip

A smaller down payment is not free money. FHA loans carry mortgage insurance premiums for most or all of the loan term. Low down payment conventional loans carry private mortgage insurance until you reach roughly 20% equity. VA loans typically carry a funding fee, though it is waived for some veterans with service connected disabilities. None of that erases the benefit, it just means the right loan depends on your full picture, not only the down payment line.

The point

People delay buying for years because they think they need six figures sitting in a bank account first. Most of the time they do not. They need the right loan program and someone willing to run the actual numbers instead of repeating the 20% rule everyone assumes is true.

Do I really not need 20% down to buy a house?

Correct. That is a guideline for skipping mortgage insurance on a conventional loan, not a minimum requirement to buy. FHA, certain conventional programs, and VA loans all allow far less down.

What credit score do I need for a 3.5% down FHA loan?

A credit score of 580 or higher qualifies for 3.5% down. Between 500 and 579, FHA still allows financing but requires 10% down instead.

Is the 3% down conventional loan available to everyone?

Not automatically. It usually comes through specific first time buyer programs like HomeReady or Home Possible, which carry their own income and eligibility rules. A lender can tell you in one conversation whether you qualify.

Who qualifies for a 0% down VA loan?

Veterans, active duty service members, and eligible surviving spouses. It is one of the strongest benefits in the mortgage world, and it applies to more people than usually realize.
